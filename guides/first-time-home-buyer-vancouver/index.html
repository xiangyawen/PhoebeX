Where should a first-time home buyer begin?

Start by checking what financing may be available and what monthly spending your household can comfortably carry. Then allow for closing costs, moving and a reserve before choosing a price range. Leave room for life after moving instead of stretching every part of your budget to the limit.

How much can you comfortably spend?

Separate your cash into a down payment, closing costs and money you keep after moving. Write out mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, utilities and any strata fees. Add everyday spending and a maintenance buffer. A mortgage calculator can help compare scenarios, but it cannot approve a loan.

Discuss financing with a lender or mortgage broker early. Canada’s financial consumer agency explains that preapproval is not a guarantee of final mortgage approval. The actual property and your circumstances still matter.

What happens between planning and moving in?

  1. Set priorities. List your work location, accessibility needs, school or childcare requirements, timing and non-negotiables.
  2. Understand representation. Review the services, term and payment arrangements in your agreement. BCFSA’s buyer agency guide explains why the agreement matters; do not assume the seller always covers every buyer cost.
  3. View and compare. Walk or drive around the neighbourhood. Try your everyday routes and compare parking, privacy, daylight and the maintenance you would take on.
  4. Discuss the offer. Ask your real estate professional and lender about financing, inspection and document-review conditions appropriate to the purchase. Their wording and deadlines matter.
  5. Prepare to complete. Confirm the remaining funds and costs with your lender and lawyer or notary, and arrange insurance to begin when required.

What should you notice during a viewing?

During a viewing, notice where daylight reaches the rooms you will use. Listen for traffic, look at visible drainage and consider the work the yard needs. These observations help you prepare questions for an inspection; a compass direction on a listing cannot tell you all of this.

An apparently newer home is not automatically problem-free. BCFSA’s inspection guide explains why removing an inspection condition can leave a buyer exposed to defects they did not discover.

First-time buyer questions

Is my down payment the only cash I need?

No. Ask for an estimate of legal or notary fees, taxes, closing adjustments, inspections, moving costs and any commission you may owe under your agreement. Keep some cash available after the purchase.

Do first-time buyers automatically get a tax exemption?

No. The BC first-time home buyers’ program has eligibility requirements. Ask your conveyancing professional to confirm which rules apply to you and the property before relying on a saving.

Can I remove a financing condition because I have preapproval?

Preapproval alone is not final financing approval. Discuss the actual home and your financial circumstances with your lender, and understand the consequences of any conditions you waive.
